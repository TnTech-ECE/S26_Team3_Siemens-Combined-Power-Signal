  \begin{block}{Abstract}

    This project aims to design a bias tee in such a way that a $2.5\ MHz$ clock signal, $200\ kHz$ communication signal, and $100\ W$ at $48\ V$ DC can be transmitted up to $10\ m$ using a single coaxial cable from a transmitting board (Tx) to a receiving board (Rx). A function generator is used to generate a $2.5\ MHz$ sine wave that acts as the clock. The system utilizes the bias tee and other various filtering techniques to allow transmission of the desired signals. Additionally, after the signals are received on the Rx board, they are amplified using an op-amp, fed into a comparator that converts the sine wave signal to a square wave that a Phase Locked Loop (PLL) IC, the Si5345, can use as a reference to generate a 25 MHz clock signal as well as a 2.5 MHz clock signal to compare the signal generation to the output clock. Unfortunately, due to issues with assembly, we were unable to utilize the PLL IC.

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Design}
    There are three main components to this project: 1.) Bias Tee circuit for coupling/decoupling AC signal with DC power including simulations and component selection; 2.) Use of jitter cleaning PLL to measure high-fidelity jitter measurements; 3.) Back-Channel communications path for debugging/troubleshooting system synchronization messages. The design is further split up into five subsystems: the Bias Tee, IC Power, Clock and Jitter Measurements, Cable, and Communications. \textbf{The Bias Tee} subsystem is responsible for the combination and separation of the power, clock, and communications signals. Its design is made up of three steps of filtering mirrored on the receiving and transmitting sides. The \textbf{IC Power} Subsystem consists of one buck converter (LT80602) that steps down the $48\ V$ input voltage to $12\ V$, and two LDOs (MAX6793 and MAX6795) to step down the voltage from $12\ V$ to $5\ V$, $3.3\ V$, and $1.8\ V$. This provides the required voltage for the other ICs in the system. The \textbf{Clock Generation and Jitter Measurement} subsystem conditions the 2.5 MHz clock signal provided by the bias tee subsystem using an ADA4899-1 opamp and an ADCMP605 comparator into a differential clock suitable for the Skyworks Si5345B jitter attenuator. This IC, configured at startup by an STM32G030K8T6 MCU, both cleans the reference signal for use outside of the system and generates a 25 MHz signal to enable jitter characterization. The \textbf{Cable} subsystem selected an RG8, $50\ \Omega$ coaxial cable because it ensures that the cable could reliably transmit a $2.5\ MHz$ clock signal and $100\ W$ DC power. It does this while minimizing losses in the clock due to reflections and safely handling the required current to transmit the DC power. The \textbf{Communications} subsystem is designed to provide effective debug communications through the high-power transmission line. This subsystem implemented the ST7580 Power Line Communications Modem to transmit 8-PSK modulated packets. This provides an effective and reliable platform for debug communication with the detectors.

    The team was tasked with having a $30\ mV$ ripple voltage across the DC output. The actual results found that the ripple was $500\ mV$ at a $1.3 kHz$ frequency and $38\ mV$ at a $400\ kHz$ frequency. The $38\ mV$ ripple is due to the switching frequency of the buck converter and the DC power supply. The source of the $500\ mV$ ripple could not be determined.

    \sectionHeading{Bitrate}

    With the Power Line Modems connected to the RX and TX boards, 998 out of 1005 test packets were successfully sent, for a packet failure rate of 0.70\% with a top measured bitrate of 4.54 kbps.
    
  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Future Improvements}
    
    \sectionHeading{Component Selection and Layout}
    Instead of using an LDO to step down $12\ V$ to $5\ V$, utilize a buck converter to step it down to that voltage first and clean the signal to provide correct voltage. Then use an LDO to step down from $5\ V$ to $3.3\ V$ and $1.8\ V$. Improvements would include changing the layout of the board to accommodate EMI standards, even though it was not specified in the objectives.

    \sectionHeading{Bias Tee}
    During testing, the team found that there was a large transient voltage across the input of the communications when the power supply was turned on and off. In order to fix this, a flyback diode could be added to disperse the DC voltage before it reaches the communications system.

    \sectionHeading{Assembly}
    Due to improper assembly of the op-amp on our board, we were not able to test the PLL jitter measurements. The signal was not properly conditioned due to faulty solder joints on the board. With proper assembly, it is likely that the system would work, as our simulations indicated full functionality.

  \end{block}
